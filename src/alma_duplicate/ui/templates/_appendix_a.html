Appendix A: how the conditions fit together

For a non-Solar observation, duplication requires the location and angular-resolution conditions, together with a qualifying continuum or spectral-line condition, against the same other observation.

  • Location: single-field interferometry uses the other observation's half-power beam; moving objects are identified by name. For a mosaic, more than 50% of proposed pointings must fall within the half-power beam area covered by the other observation. This is a count of pointings, not a percentage of overlapping map area.
  • Angular resolution: the proposed resolution differs by a factor of ≤ 2.
  • Continuum: at least two windows each have bandwidth > 1.8 GHz; requested aggregate-bandwidth sensitivity is better by a factor of ≤ 2, and frequency is within a factor of 1.3.
  • Or spectral line: the centre of any requested FDM window is encompassed by another FDM observation, with requested per-channel sensitivity better by a factor of ≤ 2 after smoothing to the same spectral resolution. Coverage, mode and sensitivity must belong to a coherent comparison.
  • Solar: observations are exempt from duplication checking.

When both purposes are selected, the report shows independent branch results; it does not require both spectral routes to pass or combine evidence from different observations.

The search scope controls candidate retrieval; the scientific position condition is evaluated separately.

These are the policy conditions. Available evaluation methods and missing evidence are shown separately in the report. Selecting a purpose does not establish that its conditions are met.
